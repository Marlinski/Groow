Learning

Every mechanism on this page ends in the same place: a gradient step on the plastic overlay. What differs is who supplies the signal, and what guards against learning the wrong thing.

Two speeds of memory

The base is a dense fp16 transformer (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507). Frozen during the day. The overlay is a LoRA adapter on every linear projection of every layer: rank 32, about 66M parameters, fp32 master weights, AdamW state kept across sessions. All learning writes into the overlay. At night the overlay is consolidated: W ← W + (α/r)·B·A per layer, an exact operation, then a blank overlay opens. Until then, deleting state/plastic/ is a full undo.

Why not train the base directly: 4B parameters with Adam needs ~64 GB of state and fp16 master weights that drift. Why not quantise: bitsandbytes is the only option on Volta, it is slower than fp16, and merging a delta into ternary or int4 weights is lossy. The overlay is the honest compromise on this card.

Passive learning: every turn

After each main-thought turn the whole exchange is rendered with the model's own chat template and trained on once. Tokens are weighted by the role that produced them, so the loss mask follows the conversation exactly as generation saw it.

RoleWeightEffect
user0.5absorb what people state (facts land through conversation)
assistant1.0reinforce its own answers, tool-call habits, style (self-distillation)
tool0tool output is transient
system0never memorised

Each step also replays two older episodes drawn from memory, preferring turns rated /good twice as often and never those rated /bad. This rehearsal is the cheapest known defence against catastrophic forgetting. The training sample is capped at train_max_len (4k tokens, tail of the conversation) independently of the 32k prompt budget.

Feedback. /good replays the turn three times at double assistant weight. /bad applies an unlikelihood loss to the assistant tokens, pushing that answer down, and bars the episode from rehearsal.

Learning is a meta-process

Groow does not operate its own learning and cannot rate itself. It acts; the limbic system decides how that felt, from free sensors (a command that failed, a call that timed out, the same call twice, a turn with no answer, a fix after a failure) and a frozen judge for the ambiguous part (how the person reacted, whether a clean-exiting command did anything). The learning passes read the conversation and the core's record of how each turn went, and write training sets to state/training/<set>.jsonl: supervised samples (messages with role weights) or rewarded samples (prompt, completion, reward, group). Only the trainer consumes them. A turn the body failed to finish is scored at nothing and never harvested: punishing the model for a brain that was not running would teach it the wrong lesson.

whenwhat is consumed
a nap, after every turnthe exchange weighted by its valence, every tool call as a credited decision, plus up to nap_max_samples pending samples
idle naps, while nobody talksthe activity log: a game's moves and rewards, a drill's scored answers
the nightfacts stated in the day's transcript, then everything pending, then replay, identity, probe, merge

Approval is late, so a turn is held until the person reacts and learned from one turn behind. A turn that went badly (repeats, failures, no answer) is recorded and never trained on. An answer the person disliked is not simply skipped: it is pushed away with an unlikelihood loss.

Late credit. Some decisions have no result at the time they are made. Asking the mentor a question is the clearest case: the receipt is not the answer. So the ask call is kept aside, and when the question resolves, answered, expired, or dropped because the inbox was full, that call is trained on then, with the outcome as its reward. The inbox is a budget (a few open questions at a time) precisely so that asking has a cost and has to be worth it.

Credit. A turn's valence is spread back over its tool calls, discounted so the last call carries most of the outcome, and each call also gets its own local signal from what came back. A failure inside an otherwise good turn stays a failure: positive spillover onto a failed call is suppressed. This is why getting better at the shell, at a game, or at an API discovered this morning is the same mechanism with no new code.

Self-play with rules as the teacher

A game is anything with rules that can score an action without a human (groow/games/: tic-tac-toe, arithmetic; the model may invent more when allowed). Each round runs 16 episodes in lockstep with batched generation. Every decision gets its reward from the rules once the episode ends; rewards are normalised across the round into advantages (GRPO-style); one REINFORCE step raises the log-probability of good decisions and lowers bad ones.

A quarter of decisions are replaced by random legal moves. Without this, a small model's policy sits on one move at ~100% probability, sampling produces no diversity, and the gradient is zero. The saturation problem will recur with any new game.

A night is due every sleep_every_steps learning steps (or /sleep). groow rollback restores base.prev and discards the overlay.

Identity

There is no rigid system prompt. state/identity.md starts from a short seed (who Groow is, how it learns, that Marlinski is its owner and mentor to be asked when stuck). Groow may rewrite it with update_identity; every version is logged. At night, context distillation moves it into the weights: for sampled recent prompts the model answers with the identity in the prompt (teacher) and is trained on the same answer without it (student). The internalisation loss, the loss of the self-description when asked "who are you?" with no system prompt, fell from 3.16 to 2.68 after a single 10-step night. When it is low enough, identity_in_prompt: false removes the text and the personality stays.

Curiosity: sensing the world

When nobody has spoken for sense_idle_minutes, the main thought receives an internal impulse (not a human message) and reads the news through its own tools: news_headlines (RSS feeds, dated and sourced), read_article, then learn_fact for what is worth keeping. Only grounded text is trained in: the answer is the source's wording with its date. A fixed pipeline does the same without the model's judgement and is the fallback when the agentic pass learns nothing.

Growth

grow(rank) consolidates and attaches a wider overlay. The new part starts at zero, so the network computes the same function the instant after; it just has more directions to move in. The same zero-init principle extends to widening MLPs (Net2Net), inserting identity-initialised layers, and bolting on a vision encoder through a zero-initialised projector. Those are the next tools.

The cycle, as it runs

Four passes, none of which the mind can call. The core starts them between turns and at night, which is the point: what is learned is decided by how things went.

PassWhat it does
feelScores the turns that have ended: the free sensors, plus the frozen judge reading how a person reacted. A turn the body failed to finish is scored at nothing, because the brain being down is not the mind's mistake.
harvestTurns what was felt into samples. Only turns that went well become examples to imitate; every turn becomes a decision to reinforce or discourage.
napThe short one, between turns: feel, harvest, a little practice, no consolidation.
nightAll of it, then the overlay is merged into the base and a blank one opens. The running brain is told to pick the new weights up, or it would go on answering from the copy it loaded hours ago.

Why conversation turns share one group

A policy step needs something to compare against. A conversation turn happens once, so a group of one has an advantage of exactly zero and the step changes nothing at all: this was true of every step until it was measured. Turns are grouped together and each is measured against how turns have been going lately, which is what a baseline is for. Games keep their own per-round groups, because there the same position really was played several ways.

Checking the judge

The judge never learns, so a badly worded question to it is a permanent mistake that nothing downstream can correct. python -m groow.limbic.calibrate runs it against exchanges where we know what a person would say and fails if it is ever confidently backwards. The current wording was chosen that way: asking whether a reply responds to the question agrees with a person on every case, where asking whether it gives the information wanted scored a good answer at −0.76.
